Part 1 of 3

Fancy a game?

Friday evening. Wei arrives at George's flat with some snacks.

  1. George

    Come in, come in! Emma's already here.

    進來進來！Emma 已經到了。

  2. Wei

    Hi, everyone. Thanks for having me. I brought some snacks.

    嗨，大家好。謝謝你們邀請我。我帶了一些零食。

  3. Emma

    Oh, lovely! So, George, what are we playing tonight?

    喔，太好了！George，我們今晚玩什麼？

  4. George

    I've got loads of board games, but I was thinking Dragon Market. Fancy a game, Wei?

    我有一大堆桌遊，不過我在想玩 Dragon Market。Wei，要不要玩一局？

  5. Wei

    Sure, but I've never played it before.

    好啊，不過我從來沒玩過。

  6. Emma

    Don't worry, it's really easy. You'll pick it up in no time.

    別擔心，很簡單。你很快就會上手。

  7. George

    Right, let's set up. Emma, can you put the board in the middle?

    好，我們來把遊戲擺好。Emma，可以把圖板放中間嗎？

  8. Emma

    Sure. Wei, pick a counter. What colour do you want?

    好。Wei，挑一個棋子。你要什麼顏色？

  9. Wei

    Um, the blue one, I guess.

    嗯……藍色的吧。

  10. George

    Blue for Wei. And I'll shuffle the pack.

    藍色給 Wei。那我來洗牌。

Part 2 of 3

How do you play?

George explains the rules, and Wei hears “go” used in a new way.

  1. Wei

    So, can you explain the rules?

    所以，可以解釋一下規則嗎？

  2. George

    Okay. On your go, you roll the dice and move your counter.

    好。輪到你的時候，你擲骰子，然後移動你的棋子。

  3. Wei

    Sorry, my... go?

    不好意思，我的……go？

  4. Emma

    Your turn. We say “go” as well.

    就是 turn，輪到你。我們也會說 go。

  5. Wei

    Ah, got it.

    喔，懂了。

  6. George

    If you land on a market square, you draw a card from the pack.

    如果你停在市集格，就從牌堆抽一張牌。

  7. Emma

    And you can swap cards with other players, if they agree.

    而且只要對方同意，你可以跟別人換牌。

  8. George

    Every card is worth points. First to ten points wins.

    每張牌都有分數。先拿到十分的人贏。

  9. Emma

    Oh, and watch out for the red squares. If you land on one, you miss a go.

    喔，還有小心紅色的格子。停在上面的話，就要暫停一回合。

  10. Wei

    Right. Roll, move, draw a card... Okay, I think I've got it.

    好。擲骰子、移動、抽牌……好，我想我懂了。

  11. George

    Great. Emma, you deal. Five cards each.

    很好。Emma，你發牌。每人五張。

  12. Emma

    And no peeking at other people's hands!

    還有，不准偷看別人的手牌！

Part 3 of 3

Best of three?

The last round. Wei needs just one more point.

  1. Emma

    Right, whose go is it?

    好，輪到誰了？

  2. George

    Mine. Hang on... Emma, were you looking at my cards?

    我。等一下……Emma，你剛剛在偷看我的牌嗎？

  3. Emma

    No! You're holding them the wrong way round.

    才沒有！是你把牌拿反了。

  4. George

    Hmm. I'm watching you, you cheat.

    哼。我在盯著你喔，你這個作弊鬼。

  5. Wei

    Okay, my go. I've got nine points. If I draw a gold card, I win.

    好，換我。我有九分。只要抽到金色的牌，我就贏了。

  6. Wei

    Four... market square! And it's... gold! Ten points!

    四……市集格！然後是……金色的！十分！

  7. Emma

    No way! Well played, Wei!

    不會吧！打得好，Wei！

  8. George

    Beginner's luck. That's all it was.

    新手運而已啦。

  9. Emma

    Don't be a sore loser, George. Last week it was a draw, and you complained all night.

    別輸不起嘛，George。上星期是平手，你就抱怨了一整晚。

  10. George

    Fine. Rematch? Best of three?

    好啦。再來一場？三戰兩勝？

  11. Wei

    I'd love to, but it's nearly eleven. Next week?

    我很想，不過快十一點了。下星期？

  12. George

    Deal. Come on, let's tidy it away.

    一言為定。來吧，把遊戲收好。

  13. Wei

    Thanks, guys. That was really fun.

    謝謝大家，今晚真的很好玩。

Reading 1 · Wei's diary

My first game night

472 words · about 3 min · read by Wei

Click any sentence to hear it.

Tonight I went to my first game night at George's flat. On the way there, I was a little nervous. I didn't know Emma very well, and I had never played Dragon Market before. So I stopped at a corner shop and bought some snacks, just in case.

今晚我去 George 家，參加了人生第一次桌遊之夜。去的路上我有點緊張。我跟 Emma 不太熟，也從來沒玩過 Dragon Market。所以我在街角的小店買了些零食，以防萬一。

When I arrived, George opened the door before I could even knock. “Come in, come in!” he said. Emma was already sitting on the sofa, and she waved at me. I gave George the snacks, and I said, “Thanks for having me.” My English teacher once told me that this is the polite thing to say when you visit someone's home. It worked! Everyone smiled, and I felt much more relaxed.

我到的時候，還沒來得及敲門，George 就把門打開了。「進來進來！」他說。Emma 已經坐在沙發上，還對我揮了揮手。我把零食交給 George，說了一句 “Thanks for having me.”。我的英文老師曾經說過，去別人家作客時這樣說很有禮貌。果然有用！大家都笑了，我也放鬆了很多。

George has loads of board games. There's a whole shelf of them in his living room, and he looked very proud of it. We chose Dragon Market because Emma said it was easy to learn, and she was right. George explained the rules in about five minutes. On your go, you roll the dice and move your counter. If you land on a market square, you draw a card. Every card is worth points, and the first person to reach ten points wins.

George 有一大堆桌遊，客廳裡整整一層架子都是，他看起來很得意。我們選了 Dragon Market，因為 Emma 說它很好學，她說得沒錯。George 大概五分鐘就把規則講完了：輪到你的時候，擲骰子、移動棋子；如果停在市集格，就抽一張牌。每張牌都有分數，先拿到十分的人贏。

I learned a lot of new words tonight, and not only about games. When George said, “It's your go,” I had no idea what he meant. In my English classes back home, we always said, “It's your turn.” Emma told me that British people use both. After that, I started to notice other differences. George called the little pieces counters, and he said pack of cards, not “deck.” At the end of the night, he asked us to tidy the game away. I think it just means “put it away,” but it sounds much more polite.

今晚我學到很多新字，而且不只是跟遊戲有關的。George 說 “It's your go” 的時候，我完全不知道他在說什麼。在台灣上英文課時，我們都說 “It's your turn”。Emma 告訴我，英國人兩種都會用。之後我開始注意到其他差異。George 把小棋子叫做 counters，說 pack of cards 而不是 deck。最後他還叫我們 tidy the game away。我想意思就是 put it away，但聽起來客氣多了。

The game itself was really fun. For the first half hour, I was losing badly. I landed on a red square twice and missed a go both times. George laughed at me every time, but I didn't mind. Emma kept giving me little tips, like which cards were worth swapping. Slowly, I started to catch up.

遊戲本身真的很好玩。前半小時我輸得很慘。我兩次停在紅色格子上，兩次都暫停一回合。George 每次都笑我，不過我不介意。Emma 一直給我一些小建議，像是哪些牌值得拿去換。慢慢地，我開始追上來了。

Then, right at the end, something amazing happened. I had nine points, and I needed one gold card to win. I rolled a four, landed on a market square and drew a card. It was gold! Emma shouted, “No way!” and George just stared at the board. He said it was only beginner's luck. Maybe he's right, but I still won.

然後就在最後，發生了一件很神奇的事。我有九分，只差一張金色的牌就贏了。我擲出四，停在市集格，抽了一張牌。是金色的！Emma 大叫「不會吧！」，George 只是盯著圖板看。他說那只是新手運。也許他說得對，但我還是贏了。

George wanted a rematch straight away, best of three, but it was nearly eleven, so we agreed to play again next week. Walking home, I noticed that I had spoken English all evening without worrying about it. That has never happened to me before. I think game night might be the best English lesson I've ever had.

George 想馬上再比一場，三戰兩勝，可是已經快十一點了，所以我們約好下星期再玩。走路回家的時候，我發現自己整晚都在講英文，卻沒有為這件事緊張。這種事以前從來沒發生過。我想，桌遊之夜可能是我上過最好的一堂英文課。

Reading 2 · A little history

Four thousand years of board games

512 words · about 3 min · read by Emma

Click any sentence to hear it.

People have been playing board games for a very long time, much longer than most of us imagine. Long before phones, televisions or even paper, people sat down together, threw dice or sticks, and tried to beat each other.

人類玩桌遊的歷史非常久，比大多數人想像的還要久。早在手機、電視，甚至紙出現之前，人們就會坐在一起，擲骰子或木棒，想辦法打敗對方。

One of the oldest games we know is the Royal Game of Ur. In the 1920s, a British archaeologist called Leonard Woolley found several game boards in the Royal Cemetery at Ur, an ancient city in what is now southern Iraq. The boards are about 4,500 years old. Each one has twenty squares, and some of them are decorated with a flower-like pattern called a rosette.

我們知道最古老的遊戲之一，是「烏爾王族局戲」（Royal Game of Ur）。1920 年代，英國考古學家 Leonard Woolley 在烏爾的王族墓地找到了好幾塊遊戲圖板。烏爾是一座古城，位置在今天的伊拉克南部。這些圖板大約有 4,500 年歷史。每塊都有二十個格子，其中幾格裝飾著像花一樣的圖案。

For a long time, nobody knew how to play it. Then, in the early 1980s, Irving Finkel, a curator at the British Museum, studied a clay tablet written by a Babylonian astronomer in 177 BC. The tablet described the rules. It turned out to be a race game: two players race their pieces from one end of the board to the other.

很長一段時間，沒有人知道這個遊戲怎麼玩。直到 1980 年代初期，大英博物館的館員 Irving Finkel 研究了一塊泥板，那是一位巴比倫天文學家在西元前 177 年寫下的。泥板上記載著遊戲規則。原來這是一種競速遊戲：兩個玩家比賽，看誰先把棋子從圖板的一端走到另一端。

In ancient Egypt, people played a game called senet. Its board has thirty squares, in three rows of ten. A painting in the tomb of an official named Hesy, made more than 4,500 years ago, shows a senet board. Much later, when the young king Tutankhamun was buried, several game boxes were placed in his tomb. Over time, senet became connected with the journey to the afterlife.

古埃及人玩一種叫 senet 的遊戲。它的圖板有三十格，排成三排、每排十格。一位名叫 Hesy 的官員墓中有一幅畫，畫的就是 senet 圖板，距今超過 4,500 年。很久以後，年輕的法老圖坦卡門下葬時，墓裡也放了好幾個遊戲盒。慢慢地，senet 被人們跟前往來世的旅程連結在一起。

Chess has travelled a long way too. Most historians believe it grew out of an Indian game called chaturanga, around the sixth century AD. The game moved to Persia, spread across the Islamic world, and reached Europe through Spain and Sicily. The rules we use today were settled in Europe at the end of the fifteenth century.

西洋棋也走過很長的路。大多數歷史學家認為，它來自一種叫做 chaturanga 的印度遊戲，大約在西元六世紀。這個遊戲傳到波斯，再傳遍伊斯蘭世界，然後經由西班牙和西西里島傳進歐洲。今天用的規則，是十五世紀末在歐洲確定下來的。

Snakes and Ladders, a favourite with children all over the world, has a long history too. It comes from an Indian game called Moksha Patam, which was used to teach lessons about good and bad behaviour. Climbing a ladder was like doing something good, and sliding down a snake was like doing something bad. The game reached Britain in the 1890s, and the early British versions still carried lessons, but with their own ideas of right and wrong. Most modern versions have no lessons at all: they're simply a game of luck.

蛇梯棋（Snakes and Ladders）是全世界小朋友都喜歡的遊戲，它的歷史也很長。它來自一款叫 Moksha Patam 的印度遊戲，當時是用來教人分辨好行為和壞行為的。爬上梯子就像做了好事，順著蛇滑下去就像做了壞事。這個遊戲在 1890 年代傳到英國，早期的英國版本還保留著教訓，只是換成了英國人自己的是非觀念。現在大多數版本已經沒有任何教訓，純粹是靠運氣的遊戲。

Some modern games have surprising stories. In 1904, an American woman called Lizzie Magie received a patent for a game called The Landlord's Game. She wanted to show how renting out land made landlords rich and tenants poor. Players kept making their own versions of her game, and one of these later became Monopoly, which Parker Brothers started selling in 1935. Parker Brothers also bought Magie's patent. Today, many people play Monopoly without knowing that it began as a lesson about unfair rents.

有些現代遊戲背後的故事很令人意外。1904 年，一位名叫 Lizzie Magie 的美國女性，為一款叫 The Landlord's Game（地主遊戲）的遊戲拿到專利。她想讓人看到：出租土地如何讓地主變富、讓房客變窮。玩家們不斷做出自己的版本，其中一個後來變成了大富翁（Monopoly），Parker Brothers 公司在 1935 年開始販售。Parker Brothers 也買下了 Magie 的專利。今天很多人玩大富翁，卻不知道它一開始是一堂關於不公平租金的課。

So the next time you roll the dice at a friend's house, remember that you're doing something people have enjoyed for thousands of years. The boards and the rules have changed, but the fun of winning, losing and asking for a rematch has stayed the same.

所以下次你在朋友家擲骰子的時候，記得你正在做一件人類已經玩了幾千年的事。圖板和規則都變了，但贏、輸、吵著要再比一場的樂趣，一直都沒變。
